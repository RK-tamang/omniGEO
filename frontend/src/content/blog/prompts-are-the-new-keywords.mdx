Keyword research starts with a database. Prompt research starts with a conversation.

That difference matters more than it sounds. A keyword is a compressed query — “crm for agencies” — stripped of context by the search box. A prompt is the full thought: *“We’re a 15-person agency and our client reporting is a mess. Which CRM should we use, and will it handle multiple brands?”* The answer engines see the second version, and it changes which sources get retrieved.

## Why one-off checks mislead

Checking ChatGPT manually is useful for a gut feel and useless as a measurement. Answers vary by session, locale, phrasing and time of day. You cannot compare last Tuesday’s screenshot to today’s and call it a trend.

A prompt set fixes the variables: same questions, same locale, same cadence, stored answers. Then movement means something.

## The four prompt types

### Category prompts

“What is the best [category] for [segment]?” These are the highest-volume, highest-value prompts. They also tend to have the most competitors and the least stable answers.

### Comparison prompts

“[Brand] vs [competitor]” and “alternatives to [brand]”. If you are absent here, you lose buyers who are already shortlisting. If you are present but mischaracterised, you lose them slightly later.

### Problem prompts

“How do I [solve problem] without [constraint]?” These surface earlier in the journey. They are easier to win, and they feed the category prompts later.

### Brand prompts

“What is [brand] known for?” and “Is [brand] good for [use case]?” These tell you whether the model’s understanding of you matches your positioning. They are diagnostic, not growth drivers.

## A sample set for a B2B SaaS company

| Type | Example prompt | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Category | “Best project management tool for design agencies in 2026” | Direct shortlist entry |
| Category | “Affordable project management software for a 10-person team” | Price-sensitive segment |
| Comparison | “[You] vs [Competitor A]” | Bottom-funnel defence |
| Comparison | “Alternatives to [Competitor B] for agencies” | Conquest opportunity |
| Problem | “How do I stop client projects slipping without hiring a PM?” | Early-stage demand |
| Brand | “What is [You] known for?” | Positioning accuracy |

Aim for 40–60 prompts to start. Fewer than 30 and the mention rate swings wildly; more than 100 and the operational cost outruns the insight.

## How often to run them

- **Weekly** for category and comparison prompts. Those are the ones buyers see.
- **Monthly** for problem prompts. They move slowly.
- **Monthly** for branded prompts, plus after any repositioning.

Keep locale and device constant. If you serve multiple markets, treat each as its own prompt set — “best CRM” in Bengaluru and in Berlin are different questions with different competitive sets.

## Reading the results

Do not stop at “mentioned / not mentioned.” For each answer, record:

1. **Position** — first recommendation, included in a list, or buried?
2. **Sentiment** — recommended, neutral, or warned against?
3. **Citations** — which domains were used to justify the claim?
4. **Competitors named** — who took the slot?

The citation list is the most actionable part. If the answer cites two review sites and a Reddit thread, those are the places to be present and accurate. If it cites a competitor’s comparison page, you have a content gap with a clear brief.

## Refresh the set every quarter

Prompts drift as your product and market change. Retire questions about features you no longer sell, add prompts for new segments, and watch for language shifts — the way buyers describe the category often changes before the category does.
